# Research index — the map to the research behind each topic

Raw research sweeps live in `appendix/` in the GitHub repo only. **They are not loaded in
voice.** For a walk that needs one, Bennett pre-loads it in a text session first (pastes the
full text or a walk brief). The curated reports (T-files) are loaded and win on conflicts.

**Tags used across all files:**
- [V] = source page read
- [V-repo] = read or counted directly from the openai/math GitHub repository
- [S] = search snippet or secondary aggregator only
- [U] = unconfirmed Chinese journal or state-media claim
- [M] or [recall] = memory, not checked
- [claim] = allegation or theory

## Topic 13 — OpenAI math release (researched 7 Oct 2026)

| Appendix | Sweep | What it covers | Reliability |
|---|---|---|---|
| A5 | What shipped | Repo structure and counts (cloned and counted); the blog post (read via a proxy); the process; caveats; OpenAI's plans; press coverage | Highest: primary sources |
| A6 | Headline credibility | Each headline problem explained; the Lean statements read one by one; expert reactions; what Lean does and does not guarantee | High on Lean statements; reactions [S] |
| A7 | Context and implications | 2026 timeline (First Proof, unit-distance, Astra, Navier–Stokes, the Fields letter, AGMAI); refereeing; arXiv policy; crypto; the AI race; motives | Mostly secondary aggregators; openai.com blocked |

### Threads worth talking through
1. **What a Lean proof is worth.** It checks the logic exactly, but the formal statement can
   quietly differ from the English headline. Families 197 (torsion) and 287 (bespoke
   definitions) are the worked examples. Analogy: a calibrated instrument versus the
   interpretation of its reading.
2. **The quasi-Riemann result.** Why a fixed zero-free line at 7/8 matters for the primes,
   why nobody had any fixed line before, and why the companion paper's weak Siegel-zero bound
   is an oddity.
3. **The curve.** From IMO gold in July 2025 to 722 papers in October 2026, and how benchmarks
   saturated on the way. Is this the "automated researcher" arriving? OpenAI's roadmap: the
   intern in Sept 2026, the researcher in March 2028.
4. **The sociology fight.** Fields Medalists' letter versus "dump it all" (Litt, Buzzard)
   versus "receipts" (Sutherland). Tao on "harvesting" open problems and Goodhart's law. AI
   labs as competitors of the researchers they sell tools to (the Buckmaster allegation).
5. **Publishing.** A corporate author on GitHub with no DOI and no referees. arXiv's
   one-strike rule. The likely two-tier literature.
6. **Why release now?** Priority claims, rebuilding credibility after the 2025 overclaim,
   recruiting, and IPO timing (timing only; no source argues it).
7. **Spillover.** Into physics (amplitudes, spin glasses, fluid blow-up), and what it does not
   do (crypto). It also links to topic 12's AI-for-physics conclusion.

### What the research could not find
- Any technical verdict from a named expert on any single result.
- Reactions from Quanta, MathOverflow, the Lean Zulip, Ellenberg, Riehl, Kalai or Aaronson;
  Nature was paywalled.
- Responses from DeepMind, Anthropic or Chinese labs to this release.
- Any journal, AMS or IMU policy on bulk AI-generated proofs.
- Per-result compute, prompts, or the failure rate between the 4,000 problems and 372
  families.
- The manuscripts themselves. No PDFs were read; the Lean files and repo metadata were.
